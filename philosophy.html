Our philosophy

Most kids don't dislike math. They dislike the version they were given. Here is what we do instead, and why.

What we believe Six ideas

Six things we believe

Each one comes with a handshake puzzle, the same one in our logo. Every kid shakes hands with every other kid once. Add one more kid and count the handshakes.

  1. 1Start with a puzzle

    We do the kind of math nobody assigns: puzzles, games and problems worth arguing about. A good problem gets a kid talking before anyone has taught them a method, and the method means more once they have needed it.

    Library events are a couple of hours of puzzles, games and problems, with free snacks and prizes.

  2. 2The talent is already here

    A local school eliminated its gifted program, and after that a kid around here who loved math had nowhere to take it. The talent stayed. The path went away. We think the ability in a rural county is the same as anywhere else. What it lacks is a harder problem and someone to work on it with.

    Two students who come to the Puzzle Circle have been named National Math Stars.

  3. 3Free means free

    No family pays for anything, and nobody who teaches is paid. Libraries lend the rooms. Every dollar we raise goes to books, competition fees and the ride to the test, and we publish the budget so anyone can check.

    Cost to any family: $0. See the budget.

  4. 4Students teach students

    Our instructors are high school students from Albany and Athens. A kid learns differently from someone a few years older who was stuck on the same problem not long ago. New instructors learn alongside experienced ones, and a professor at Ohio University and other doctoral advisors guide the team.

    More than a dozen volunteer instructors. Meet the team.

  5. 5Anyone can walk in

    You don't need to be a "math kid," sign up, or have come before. If your child is anywhere between bored in math class and obsessed with it, there is a seat. Bring a friend. Grown-ups are welcome at the table too.

    Sign-up for library events: none. Just turn up.

  6. 6Then a harder problem

    Once a kid is hooked, they should have somewhere to go. Math Captains stays small on purpose so every student gets real attention, and each session is a little harder than the last. For most of them, Math Kangaroo will be the first competition they have entered.

    Ten Math Captains this year. First stop: Math Kangaroo, March 18, 2027.
