Free math for kids in Athens County.

We meet at the library and do the kind of math nobody assigns: puzzles, games, and problems worth arguing about. It costs nothing, and everyone who teaches is a volunteer.

  • Seven free events last year, four at the Wells Library in Albany
  • Over ten volunteer instructors from local high schools
  • Advised by an Ohio University math professor and several PhD students

Try one

Ten kids show up to Puzzle Club. Every kid shakes hands with every other kid exactly once. How many handshakes happen?

45. Each kid shakes nine hands, which gives ninety. But that counts every handshake twice, once from each side, so halve it. This is the same reasoning that later gets called a combination, and a nine-year-old can get there without the word.

Two ways to take part

Library events

Open to anyone, no sign-up, free

A couple of hours of puzzles, games, and problems, with a separate track for elementary kids and for middle and high schoolers. Bring a friend. You do not need to be good at math, and you do not need to have come before.

The cohort

Ten students, by application

A small group that meets online between sessions and in person once a month, working through real problem-solving and heading to the Math Kangaroo competition in March. For most of them it will be the first competition they have ever entered.

Why this exists

Alexander eliminated its gifted program. After that, a kid around here who loved math had nowhere to take it. Not a team, not a club, not a competition. The talent did not go anywhere, but the path did.

So a few of us built one. Two students who come to Puzzle Club have since been named National Math Stars, which is roughly the point: the ability was already here, sitting in ordinary classrooms in a rural county, waiting for somebody to hand it a harder problem.

We think most kids do not dislike math. They dislike the version of it they were given.

Who runs it

  • More than a dozen volunteer instructors, students at Albany and Athens high schools.
  • Senior advisors including a mathematics professor at Ohio University and several PhD students.
  • Nobody is paid. Every event is free to every family, and always will be.

Help out

Teach

If you are a high school student who likes math, you can run a table at an event. You do not need to be the best mathematician in the room. You need to be able to ask a good question and then wait.

Fund

Our entire year costs about $2,650, which covers workbooks, competition registration fees, and laptops for students who do not have one at home. That is roughly $265 per student. If your organization funds this kind of work, we would like to talk.
