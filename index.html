Puzzle 1 · Warm-up

Unsolved

Ten kids show up to the Albany-Athens Puzzle Circle. Every kid shakes hands with every other kid exactly once. How many handshakes happen?

45. Each kid shakes nine hands, which gives ninety. But that counts every handshake twice, once from each side, so halve it. This is the same reasoning that later gets called a combination, and a nine-year-old can get there without the word.

Puzzle 2 · Patterns

Unsolved

What number comes next in this pattern?

  • 2
  • 6
  • 12
  • 20
  • ?

30. The gaps grow by two each time: 4, 6, 8, and so the next gap is 10. Another way to see it: 1×2, 2×3, 3×4, 4×5, and next comes 5×6.

Puzzle 3 · Logic

Unsolved

A farm has chickens and cows. Together they have 20 heads and 56 legs. How many cows are on the farm?

8 cows. If all 20 animals were chickens there would be 40 legs, but there are 56, so 16 legs are missing. Each cow swaps a chicken’s 2 legs for 4, adding 2, so 16 ÷ 2 = 8 cows (and 12 chickens).

Circle complete! That is exactly how a Puzzle Circle event feels. Come to the next one and bring a friend.

For parents and teachers

What families ask us most.

Does it cost anything?

No. Every event is free to every family, and always will be. Nobody who teaches is paid.

Do we need to sign up for a library event?

No. Library events are open to anyone with no sign-up. Turn up, or write to us first if you would rather know what to expect.

My child is not “a math kid.” Is this still for them?

Yes. If your child is somewhere between bored in math class and obsessed with it, this is for them. You do not need to be good at math, and you do not need to have come before.

What ages is it for?

Library events have a separate track for elementary kids and another for middle schoolers, so everyone works on problems that fit them.

Who is actually teaching?

More than a dozen volunteer instructors who are students at Albany and Athens high schools. They are advised by a mathematics professor at Ohio University and several doctorates.

What is the cohort, and how do we apply?

The cohort is a group of ten students who meet online between sessions and in person once a month, and who compete in the Math Kangaroo competition in March. Applications open once a year. Send us a note and we will let you know when.

Why this exists

The talent was already here.

A local school eliminated its gifted program. After that, a kid around here who loved math had nowhere to take it. Not a team, not a club, not a competition. The talent did not go anywhere, but the path did.

So a few of us built one. Two students who come to the Albany-Athens Puzzle Circle have since been named National Math Stars, which is roughly the point: the ability was already here, sitting in ordinary classrooms in a rural county, waiting for somebody to hand it a harder problem.

We think most kids do not dislike math. They dislike the version of it they were given.
