The Guild

Ten students from the Alexander district who want more math than school gives them. They train together all year, enter real competitions, and pay nothing.

Why it exists Alexander district

A team for kids who had nowhere to take it

Alexander eliminated its gifted program. The open library events give any kid a taste of real problem solving, but a few want to keep going, and around here there was no team, club or competition for them to go to.

The Guild is that next step. It stays small on purpose, so every student gets real attention from the instructors and a seat at every competition we enter.

What students get All of it free

What every Guild student gets

Families pay nothing. Grants and donations cover all of it.

A weekly check-in
An online session every week with an instructor and the rest of the group, to work problems and talk them through.
A monthly meetup
A longer in-person session once a month at the Athens branch of the Athens County Public Library.
A laptop if they need one
A Chromebook with a case and charger for any student without a computer at home, so nobody misses the weekly check-in.
Their own workbooks
Beast Academy or Art of Problem Solving books matched to their level.
Competition entry
Registration for Math Kangaroo, paid for, plus a ride to the testing site and a meal that day.
Instructors and advisors
Student volunteer instructors from Albany and Athens high schools, backed by a mathematics professor at Ohio University and several PhD students.
Something to show for it
A Guild shirt, and a certificate and medal at the end of the year.
The plan Year one and two

The plan

  1. Once a year

    Applications open

    Students apply to join, and ten get a spot for the year. Leave your email and we'll tell you when they open.

  2. Every week

    Train together

    Online check-ins every week and an in-person session every month, working through problems a little harder than the last.

  3. March 18, 2027

    Math Kangaroo

    The year's main competition. For most of the group it will be the first math competition they have ever entered. We may also enter a team in MOEMS, the Mathematical Olympiads for Elementary and Middle Schools.

  4. Year two

    AMC 8

    Students who are ready move up to the AMC 8, the Mathematical Association of America's contest for middle school students.

Funding How you can help

How it gets paid for

We're raising the $2,650 from grants and donations, so families never pay.

  • Requested

    $750 from the Foundation for Appalachian Ohio, to buy the Chromebooks and the Zoom license.

  • Working on

    Registering the Puzzle Circle as a 501(c)(3) nonprofit, so foundations and companies can give to us directly. Until then, gifts go through a fiscal sponsor, the Friends of the Athens County Public Libraries.

  • Given in kind

    Every instructor's time and the library meeting rooms. Book publishers and local businesses can donate workbooks or supplies the same way.

  • You

    If your organization funds math or education in Appalachian Ohio, a gift of any size goes straight to the lines above. Write to us and we'll send the full budget.

What's next Nelsonville-York

Next: a second Guild in Nelsonville-York

The same program for ten more students, in the Nelsonville-York district at the north end of Athens County.

Nelsonville-York students would get the same weekly check-ins, workbooks, paid competition entry and a ride to Math Kangaroo. The second Guild costs less to start because it shares instructors, advisors and the curriculum we've already built.

We've requested $1,517 from the Nelsonville Community Foundation, part of the Foundation for Appalachian Ohio, through our fiscal sponsor Melodies 4 Medicine. Volunteer instructors, faculty advisors and library meeting space are given in kind.
